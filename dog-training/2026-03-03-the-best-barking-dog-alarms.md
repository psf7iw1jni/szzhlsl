What to Consider When Choosing a Barking Dog Alarm

There are several features to consider when shopping for the best barking dog security devices for your home. To ensure the alarm will effectively detect and dissuade people, consider the alarm’s range and sensitivity, volume level, sound modes, installation and connectivity options, and any additional features. Range and Sensitivity

The best barking dog alarm units have a reliable detection system with a high-enough sensitivity and range to detect intruders but one that won’t continually give false alarms. Many barking dog alarms are placed indoors and are able to detect movement through walls and doors to catch any intruders lurking around the home. A few units come in two pieces, with a separate sensor placed outdoors while the alarm system sits indoors.

Barking dog alarms usually have a range between 10 and 30 feet, but some devices with outdoor sensors have a much larger range of up to 1,000 feet. To choose a suitable range, consider the area that needs monitoring: Some homes need a much smaller range (for example, monitoring the front step of a townhouse), while others might require a larger range to signal any human or animal intruders wandering the yard.

An alarm that is too sensitive might disturb the household or neighbors, while one that is not sensitive enough won’t activate when it needs to. To offer more flexibility, it’s best to buy a unit with an adjustable range or sensitivity, which allows users to set their own preferences. Volume

A good alarm is loud enough to scare away animal and human intruders—broadcasting the sound through walls, windows, and doors—but quiet enough to not disturb neighbors. Many alarms advertise their decibel level, and the ideal range is between 100 and 115 decibels. This decibel level is comparable to the maximum volume of most personal listening devices or a loud entertainment venue (such as a concert or bar).

Along with a loud-enough volume, the top barking dog alarm models also have decent sound quality, so it sounds like there’s a real dog behind the gate or door. The best way to gauge the sound quality is by reading online reviews. The audio should sound realistic, and the speaker should not create any tinny, rattling, or artificial sounds. Modes

The simplest barking dog alarm system options have one mode, playing an angry dog barking sound whenever they detect motion. There are also alarms that change the volume or frequency of the barking audio as people get closer to make it sound more realistic. Other alarms have several modes to suit different uses.

Common additional modes include a doorbell mode, siren mode, and other options, such as calming nature sounds. The sharp sound of a dog barking can be startling or irritating to approaching guests or to people inside the home, so having the option for other sounds is useful. A doorbell sound or nature noises can notify occupants of someone approaching without setting off the barking audio. Installation and Connectivity

A good barking dog alarm system is easy to install: Most units are just plug-and-place options that need to be plugged into an alternating current (AC) outlet and placed in a preferred location. Some options can also be wall-mounted. While most barking dog alarms are AC-powered, a few offer a battery-only mode or the option for a battery backup in case of a power outage.

Barking dog alarms come in all-in-one units, with the sensor and alarm in one piece, or in two-piece units, with a separate sensor and alarm system. Some two-piece units allow users to place the sensor outdoors, which allows for more accurate motion detection. If the alarm is being installed outdoors, look for a waterproof or water-resistant construction (depending on whether the installation location is fully or partially exposed).
