# What You Need to Know to Safely Store Home Heating Oil - This Old House

Skip to content

Home heating oil is a common fuel source for residential heating systems, especially in areas where natural gas isn’t readily available. Storing home heating oil safely contributes to the efficiency of your heating system and helps protect the environment. Here’s everything you need to know to make sure your home heating oil is stored safely. Choosing the Right Storage Tank for Oil

Choose a storage tank for your home’s heating oil based on safety, durability, and maintenance. Steel, fiberglass, and polyethylene are the most common materials used for these tanks, each with its own advantages and disadvantages. Here’s a detailed comparison of these tank types. Steel Tanks

Steel tanks are known for their robustness and ability to withstand physical impacts. Compared to plastic tanks, they are less likely to be damaged by external forces. Steel is non-combustible and fire-resistant, making these tanks a safer option in the event of a fire. With proper maintenance, steel tanks can have a long lifespan, often lasting several decades.

However, steel tanks are prone to rust and corrosion, especially if they aren’t properly maintained or if they’re exposed to moisture. This can lead to leaks and potential environmental contamination. They’re heavy, which can make installation and relocation more difficult and expensive. While the initial purchase price may be competitive, the long-term costs of maintenance to prevent rust and corrosion can add up. Fiberglass Tanks

Fiberglass tanks don’t rust or corrode, making them ideal for long-term storage without frequent maintenance. They’re also significantly lighter than steel, making them easier and less expensive to install. Fiberglass tanks are resistant to various chemicals and environmental conditions. They often come with long warranties and can last for decades without significant degradation.

Fiberglass tanks are generally more expensive upfront compared to steel tanks. While durable, fiberglass can be brittle and may crack under severe impact or stress. Polyethylene Tanks

Like fiberglass, polyethylene tanks don’t corrode or rust, making them a low-maintenance option. Polyethylene is more flexible than fiberglass and less prone to cracking. It can absorb minor impacts without damage. These tanks are lightweight and easy to handle, which simplifies installation and reduces costs. Polyethylene tanks are often the most cost-effective option in terms of initial purchase price.

Prolonged exposure to sunlight can cause polyethylene to degrade over time, although many tanks are treated to resist UV damage. Polyethylene is combustible, which can pose a risk in the event of a fire. These tanks may not last as long as steel or fiberglass tanks, especially in harsh environmental conditions. Regular Oil Storage Maintenance and Inspection

Conduct visual inspections to check the tank, pipes, and fittings for signs of corrosion, rust, or damage. Inspect the ground around the tank for signs of leaks or spills. Schedule annual inspections by a certified technician to identify potential issues before they become serious problems. Installing a leak detection system can also provide early warning of potential leaks. Preventing Contamination

Preventing contamination helps maintain fuel quality and protect your heating system. Ensure all tank openings are properly sealed to prevent water, dirt, and debris from entering. Consider using fuel additives to prevent sludge buildup and maintain fuel quality. Regular tank cleaning is also important to remove sludge and contaminants. Handling and Refilling

Safe handling and refilling practices help prevent spills and contamination. Purchase heating oil from reputable, certified suppliers to ensure fuel quality. Use proper transfer procedures to minimize spills, and always supervise the refilling process. Have spill containment kits on hand and know how to respond to spills quickly and effectively. Legal and Environmental Considerations

Comply with all local, state, and federal regulations regarding the storage of home heating oil. Obtain necessary permits for tank installation and operation, and follow regulations regarding tank size, location, and maintenance. Report any spills or leaks immediately to the appropriate authorities and take proactive measures to prevent soil and water contamination.
