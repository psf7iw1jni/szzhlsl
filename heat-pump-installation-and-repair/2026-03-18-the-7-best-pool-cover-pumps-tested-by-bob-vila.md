What to Consider When Choosing Pool Cover Pumps

When selecting a pool cover pump, it’s crucial to consider what pump size, flow rate, and cord length are suitable for the size of the pool cover. Ahead, learn more about these and other important features to avoid making mistakes with your pool setup. Manual vs. Automatic

There are two types of pool cover pumps—manual and automatic. An automatic pool cover pump senses when there is too much water on top of the pool cover and switches on automatically to pump that excess water off the cover. This type of pump is ideal for pool owners who aren’t able to monitor their pool daily to make sure water doesn’t accumulate and damage the cover or create a drowning hazard. In contrast, an electric manual pump must be turned on manually by the pump owner to remove water from the top of the pool cover. Flow Rate

Pool cover pumps vary in terms of how much water they can pump off the cover per hour. Pool cover pump flow rates range from 250 GPH to 2,500 GPH. Choosing the right size pump depends on the surface area of the pool cover and the climate of the pool’s location. Smaller 850 GPH units are suitable as above-ground pool cover pumps , as well as for small inground pools, while larger inground pools require a pump with a flow rate of at least 1,500 GPH. Climate is also important. Pools located in regions that experience a high amount of precipitation require a pool cover pump with a higher flow rate than those located in more arid regions. Cord Length and Hose Hookup

Power cord length is also an important factor to consider when selecting a pool cover pump. The pump’s power cord should be long enough to extend from the center of the pool to the nearest outdoor GFCI power outlet. While it’s OK to use an extension cord to increase the range of the pump, the pump’s cord should be at least long enough to reach the pool deck, minimizing the chances of electric shock.

One should also consider hose length. While most pumps use a standard .75-inch garden hose to carry water from the pump to another location, others come with designated drain hoses. The drain hose should be long enough to extend into a part of the yard that directs water away from any structures and the pool. Fully Submersible

Many pool cover pumps can be fully submerged, which makes them useful not only for removing water from the pool cover but also for lowering the pool level to prevent overflow during periods of heavy precipitation. Fully submersible pumps are also quieter since the water absorbs much of the noise generated by the pump’s motor and impeller. Photo: Paul Rankin for BobVila.com FAQs

Q. How do you keep rain off your pool cover?

To keep rain off your pool cover, place the automatic pump at the center of the cover and leave it plugged in. For manual pool cover pumps, monitor the weather and use the pump after it rains to remove water from the pool cover. Q. What is the best pool cover pump?

Q. Where should a pool cover pump be placed?

Place the pool cover pump at the center of the cover, where rainwater accumulates on top of it. Q. How do you weigh down a pool cover?

The best way to weigh down the edges of a pool cover to keep it taut is to use sandbags, which are not jagged or rough like rocks or bricks, which can damage the cover.

Q. Can you fully submerge a pool cover pump?

Yes, most pool cover pumps are fully submersible, but always check the manufacturer’s instructions to be sure.

Q. Can you leave a pool cover pump on all the time?

Only if it has an automatic shutoff feature; otherwise, it can burn out or run dry and get damaged.

Q. What is the difference between a sump pump and a pool cover pump?

A sump pump is designed for basements and heavy flooding, while a pool cover pump is built to remove water from pool covers. Generally speaking, a sump pump will be much bigger and heavier, while a pool cover pump needs to be light enough to sit on top of the pool cover to siphon rainwater away. Meet the Tester
