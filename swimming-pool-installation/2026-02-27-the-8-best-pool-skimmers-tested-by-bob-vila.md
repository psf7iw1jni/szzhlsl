What to Consider When Choosing a Pool Skimmer

Before deciding on either a manual or automatic pool skimmer as a go-to pool tool, it’s good to be familiar with several other features that are frequently considered. It’s worth taking a few minutes to think about these factors, as they impact initial cost and how the pool skimmer will be used on a day-to-day basis. Type of Pool Skimmer

There are two main categories of pool skimmers: manual and automatic. Choosing the type that is most appropriate will depend on both the type of pool (above-ground or inground) and how much daily effort the pool owner wants to put in.

Manual and automatic pool skimmers vary in cost and complexity. In the following sections, we look at each of these aspects in more detail. Manual

The simplest manual pool skimmer is essentially a shallow net attached to a pole. The main advantage of manual pool skimmers is price. These are the low-budget solutions, and they do a perfectly good job of fishing a few leaves out of the water.

If they have drawbacks, it’s that they often can’t pick up very much, they may not be especially durable, and they always require physical effort. Lifting a net containing 20 or 30 pounds of wet leaves while leaning over a pool is not easy.

There are some manual skimmers that are larger and stronger than others. There are also pool maintenance kits that have skimmer heads and attachments for other pool-cleaning tasks. Automatic

Automatic pool skimmers come in three formats: stationary, floating, and robotic.

Stationary pool skimmers are fitted to the side of above-ground pools, and they are connected to the water pump/circulation system. Leaves and other debris fall into a skimmer basket on the outside of the pool or hooked to the inside rail so it is easy to empty.

Floating pool skimmers are designed to be attached to pool vacuums. While the vacuum cleans the bottom of the pool, the floating skimmer creates a whirlpool that draws in material from the surface.

Robotic pool skimmers run independently of the pool pump, and they swim across the surface, picking up floating waste as they go. A pool robot skimmer is usually a cordless, solar-powered device. While the initial investment is higher, there are no ongoing costs, and they are often environmentally friendly. Pool Type

The type of pool that needs to be cleaned will help determine which type of skimmer to purchase. Some skimmers can be used in virtually any pool, but choosing the wrong kind could damage or destroy the pool.

Above-ground pools can be cleaned with manual pool skimmers, though it can sometimes be awkward to reach all the way around a larger pool with a net. Standing on a movable step or platform will get the job done (just make sure it’s stable!). Alternatively, purchasing a longer telescopic pole can make a manual skimmer easier to use.

Stationary pool skimmers are described as suitable for above-ground pools, but it’s important to check installation requirements. Some hang or clamp a pool filter basket on the edge, while others require a hole to be cut into the side of the pool for installation.

Robotic pool skimmers work well in above-ground pools as well. While floating pool skimmers can work in above-ground pools, these skimmers need to work with a pool vacuum, and some robotic vacuums can damage soft-sided pools, so check the pool instructions before using these tools.

Inground pools can be cleaned with manual skimmers, robotic skimmers, and floating skimmers, but stationary pool skimmers are not suitable for inground pools. Most inground pools have built-in skimmer inlets connected to the pool’s pump and filter, but there’s no edge to use to connect an add-on stationary skimmer. Maintenance and Ease of Use

The majority of pool skimmers are easy to look after and require minimal maintenance. However, overlooking maintenance will usually reduce the tool’s effectiveness and shorten its useful life, so it’s worth considering basic upkeep.

With manual pool skimmers, it’s really just a question of emptying the net. The contents can usually be added to the compost heap. Because manual skimmers are very low cost, though, they are often neglected. If left lying on the lawn, someone may eventually step on it, or moisture might get into any metal parts and cause rust. Simply standing it against a wall under cover will prevent most problems.

Stationary pool skimmers are mostly made from strong plastic and, beyond emptying the basket and occasionally wiping it with a damp cloth, they are usually maintenance-free. Floating pool skimmers collect waste in the attached vacuum, requiring minimal maintenance of their own.

Robotic skimmers have an internal collecting tray that needs to be emptied. Solar panels are less effective if dusty, so wiping them off regularly will make them more effective. Capacity

The capacity of a particular pool skimmer will affect how much debris can be collected and how often it needs to be emptied.

Net size is usually the determining factor on manual pool skimmers, and some have a weight rating.

Above-ground pool skimmers have a collecting basket, so check the size of the basket before ordering.

With floating pool skimmers, it’s the vacuum that determines how much can be collected.

Robot pool skimmers have an internal tray, so check the size of the tray before purchasing.

The amount of debris collected will determine how frequently the collection tray needs to be emptied. If the pool is in an open area, for example, it will gather fewer leaves than a pool with shade trees nearby.

With most pool skimmers, it is best to empty the device as soon as the job is finished, or waste can start to rot inside the net or tray. While stationary models with collecting baskets are the exception, clearing them regularly is still a good idea. FAQs

There is a lot to consider when searching for a new pool skimmer to keep a backyard swimming pool clean, and you may have a few lingering questions. Below are answers to some frequently asked questions. Q. How long should a pool skimmer last?

How long a pool skimmer lasts very much depends on the type and how well it is looked after. Even low-cost nets will last several years if cleaned carefully, rather than being left lying around on the lawn or poolside.

It’s challenging to accurately predict the expected longevity of robotic pool skimmers, as these are relatively new. However, the experts we consulted suggest that 5 to 8 years is a reasonable estimate. Pool skimmers with baskets for above-ground pools are rated for 20 years plus.

Q. Where should a pool skimmer be placed?

For aesthetic reasons, the skimmer should be placed on the side of the pool nearest the house so it is less visible. Fixed pool skimmers need to be around half full with water, and a position facing the normal wind direction is usually a good idea, as this will help blow debris into the pool skimmer opening. However, always follow the manufacturer’s instructions for optimal performance.

Q. Do you really need a skimmer in a pool?

You certainly need a pool skimmer of some kind in order to keep pool water clean and safe for swimming. Whether you choose a manual net, a fixed option, or a robotic pool skimmer is a matter of personal choice. Check out the article above for practical guidance on choosing the best pool skimmer for your needs.

Q. How often should you use a pool skimmer?

To keep the water in pristine condition, manual pool skimmers are used once a day. It only takes a few minutes if done regularly. Daily skimming not only keeps the water clean, but it also helps prevent debris from clogging pump filters, which can cause overheating and shorten their life.

The same frequency is good for floating pool skimmers and robots. Because swimmers shouldn’t be in the water when floaters or robots are operating, many people will only run them once a week. A manual net can take care of daily chores, and it’s a very low-cost addition. Pool skimmers attached to the sides of above-ground pools run continuously whenever the pump is working. Q. How many pool skimmers do you need?

However, it’s also advised not to have too many pool skimmers; if they’re positioned too close together, they can interfere with each other’s effectiveness. Q. Are wide-mouth skimmers better?

Yes, wide-mouth skimmers can enhance surface cleaning by drawing in more debris and expanding the skimming area, especially in larger pools.

Q. Should a pool skimmer be open or closed?

A pool skimmer should generally remain open during circulation to allow it to collect debris efficiently. You should close it when you need to perform any maintenance on it.

Q. Should I run my skimmer all the time?

Running the skimmer during the pool pump’s cycle is ideal. It doesn’t need to run 24/7, but consistent daily use helps keep water clean. Meet the Tester
