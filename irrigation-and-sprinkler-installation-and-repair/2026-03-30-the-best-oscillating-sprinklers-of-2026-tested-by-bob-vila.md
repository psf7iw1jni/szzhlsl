What to Consider When Choosing an Oscillating Sprinkler

Lawn size, the adjustability of the spray pattern, water pressure demands, and sprinkler construction are some of the important factors to consider when choosing the best oscillating water sprinklers for lawns and gardens. Keep reading to learn more about these and other factors to consider when shopping for the best oscillating sprinkler. Lawn Size

Lawn size will help determine the best sprinkler, as it’s helpful to purchase a sprinkler that will minimize the number of times it must be moved to cover the entire yard. It’s also beneficial to choose a sprinkler with a spray that’s not too big so you can avoid wasteful overspray.

To determine the square footage of the yard or garden, multiply its length by its width. Then use this number—the area—to shop for the appropriately sized sprinkler. For example, a lawn that is 75 feet long by 50 feet wide would need a sprinkler that can cover at least 3,750 square feet.

Since oscillating sprinklers have a square-shaped coverage area, nonrectangular yards will require multiple sprinklers or a rotating sprinkler for full coverage. A larger yard also may need multiple sprinklers to cover the entire area in one watering session. Keep in mind that homes with water pressure below the standard 30 PSI won’t have enough pressure to meet the sprinkler’s maximum coverage standard. Material

Oscillating sprinklers for lawn watering consist of either plastic or metal parts, and each material has its pros and cons.

Metal sprinklers will better endure accidental impacts from a lawn mower or errant soccer ball and serve as a sturdier base for the oscillating sprinkler head, but they are more prone to corrosion and rust.

Sprinklers with plastic parts are more affordable but will not withstand impacts as well. If leaks occur, you may have to replace parts such as sprinkler heads or valves, if applicable, or purchase a new sprinkler.

Oscillating sprinklers use a mechanism that moves the head back and forth slowly. Sprinklers that keep this mechanism in an enclosed chamber are more durable than models that have a drive exposed to the elements.

Though this design is less expensive, the gears in an exposed drive can clog with dirt and debris, preventing the sprinkler from oscillating. With that in mind, a sprinkler with an open drive needs to be stored during inclement weather to protect these moving parts. Sled Base

Unlike other lawn sprinkler types, sled bases are not rooted to the ground by a stake, which can damage the yard. This design also makes it easy to maneuver them around the yard to cover multiple watering zones, much like a portable sprinkler system. Most sled bases are made of metal or plastic with an attachment for a standard garden hose and controls that adjust the range on one end.

Most oscillating irrigation sprinklers have a long sled base that is about 15 to 17 inches long and 5 to 6 inches wide, although some oscillating sprinklers use a sled base with a circular design that is about a foot in diameter. This large base provides a stable foundation that keeps the sprinkler steady as the head oscillates back and forth. Spray and Adjustability

Oscillating sprinklers have a series of jets typically set in a line across an oscillating bar that sprays water up into the air. The jets are set at different angles to cover the watering area evenly. Most sprinklers have between 16 and 20 jets, with the latter providing the greatest amount of coverage. These jets oscillate back and forth at a consistent speed to allow for even watering across the entire coverage area.

The spray is also adjustable. Most oscillating sprinklers have an adjuster that controls how far the sprinkler head oscillates in either direction. Higher-end sprinklers usually allow the user to adjust the coverage area’s width, and some include a zoom knob that controls the size of the coverage area’s footprint. Water Pressure

Most oscillating sprinklers base their maximum coverage on 30 PSI of water pressure, which suits a typical city water standard of about 30 to 50 PSI that most homes use. If the available water pressure is low, the sprinkler won’t be able to reach the stated maximum coverage. If the water pressure is too low, the water may not generate enough force to turn the oscillating sprinkler head. Those with homes with water pressure that falls below this 30 PSI threshold may want to consider a sprinkler designed for low pressure.

High water pressure can also have a negative impact on lawn sprinkler oscillating performance, causing the water to mist as it exits the nozzles. A pressure regulator attached between the garden hose and the sprinkler can reduce this pressure. The best way to determine a home’s water pressure is to use a water gauge or flow meter. Water Conservation

Since an oscillating sprinkler can use about 17 gallons of water per minute, it’s important to look for features that help direct as much of that water to nourish the lawn as possible. Oscillating sprinklers cover a fairly precise area, making it easy to position them to water desired areas and only those areas. This helps conserve water and prevents wasteful watering of driveways and sidewalks. Those utilizing water from a pond or creek may need a sprinkler pump to facilitate proper water flow.

Some sprinklers include a built-in mechanical timer. A sprinkler with a timer allows the user to set the sprinkler to shut off after a set amount of time, making it a great solution for preventing overwatering for those who don’t have a sprinkler control system. Convenience

Some oscillating sprinkler models include features that make them more convenient to use. This includes adapters that attach to the hose end, which make attaching and detaching the sprinkler easier. They eliminate the need to screw and unscrew the connector when it’s time to store the hose or the sprinkler.

Most oscillating sprinklers also include built-in strainers, which prevent debris from entering the sprinkler and clogging the nozzles. Higher-end oscillating sprinklers also have flow controls that allow the user to adjust water output. Some sprinklers include built-in timers, which will shut off the sprinkler after a preset period of time, preventing overwatering.

When faced with expensive sprinkler repair costs, the convenience and low cost of oscillating sprinklers can get you by until repairs to your permanent system are made. FAQs

If you’re wondering how to best use an oscillating sprinkler, then read on for answers to some of the most commonly asked questions about these watering devices.

Q. What kind of sprinkler covers the most area?

With its ability to cover 4,973 square feet, the Eden 4,973-square-foot oscillating sprinkler provides some of the broadest coverage of this sprinkler type, making it ideal for large yards. This sprinkler is also versatile. You can adjust it to cover a much smaller footprint to avoid overspray onto driveways and sidewalks. Another solid option is the Melnor sprinkler, which can cover 4,500 square feet.

Q. How long should you water the lawn with an oscillating sprinkler?

Most oscillating sprinklers will produce about 1 inch of water per hour. At that rate, you’ll want to water your lawn for 1 hour, once a week, as most lawns need between 1 and 2 inches of water per week to stay healthy. When coming up with a weekly plan for how long to water your lawn, be sure to account for any rainfall and adjust the amount of time you run your oscillating sprinkler accordingly.

Q. How do I adjust my oscillating sprinklers?

Oscillating sprinklers have adjusters that allow you to set how far the sprinkler head oscillates back and forth. After locating the adjusters on the sprinkler, slide the adjusters toward the sprinkler head to shorten the range or farther away from the head to broaden the range. Some sprinklers also have buttons or slide adjusters on the sprinkler bar that allow you to narrow or broaden the spray pattern.

Q. How many gallons per minute does an oscillating sprinkler use?

Oscillating sprinklers can use up to 17 gallons of water per minute, depending on water pressure, hose size, and sprinkler settings. Meet the Tester
