# Maker Interview: Katie Cleveland of Addicted 2 DIY - This Old House

Jenn: Hi guys, I’m Jenn Largesse from House One and today I’m here with Katie from Addicted 2 DIY. Katie, thank you for being here. Katie: Hi! Thank you for having me.

Jenn: So I thought that we would start out and have you tell us a little bit about your blog and about how you got started.

[Katie Cleveland of](https://katie-cleveland-of.themaplelane.com/katie-cleveland-of/202608226582.html)

Katie: I got started about four and a half years ago. I started because we didn’t have any furniture in our house. We were a single income family. We had not enough money to buy anything. So I kind of pitched to my husband, “Hey, let’s build it.” We started working on projects together and then I just kind of ran away with it and started doing it on my own and decided to create a website.

Katie’s story echoes a pattern This Old House has seen again and again among DIY creators. When Kevin O’Connor visited social media woodworker Sarah of Rocky Canyon Rustic for an Ask This Old House guest build, she shared a remarkably similar origin: “I honestly got into this when we bought this house about eight, nine years ago because it was in a great location but really just needed a lot of work. And so I started doing some projects just to get things moving, and the more I did, the more I wanted to do because I really enjoyed it.” As Kevin put it, “Necessity is the mother of invention, right? Happens a lot with new homeowners.”

This Old House Magazine has profiled several DIYers who followed a similar path from necessity to passion. Jaime Costiglio, featured in the November 2018 issue, built her skills—and an online following—filling her family’s 18th-century saltbox with handmade charm. “That’s the great thing about DIY,” she told the magazine. “You always have the chance to do it over to get it exactly the way you want it.”

What is your best advice for new DIY’ers?

Katie: You know the biggest thing, and for me personally, what I was most intimidated by was the tools. There’s so many tools that you can buy and if you’ve got a huge budget, it’s easy. But for us starting out with a very small budget, really, I mean every home needs a drill. Jenn: Yep.

Katie: Circular saw can get you a long way. And there’s like the Kreg Jig, there’s several of them. And pocket joinery is really, it’s the bare bones easiest way to get started in woodworking but it’s still really a great joinery method.
