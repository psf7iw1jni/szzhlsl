Our wallets have already felt it: Prices of products in certain categories will be higher than in previous years as a result of tariffs, a tax on imported goods, which are often passed to consumers. While food, clothing, toys, and sporting goods are especially vulnerable to higher prices due to tariffs, there several home-related product categories that will also see a spike—which may strain your budget for holiday gifts this year.

According to the Federal Reserve, home décor, electronics, appliances, and furniture are all costlier than last year. You can expect to pay about 6.5 percent more for furniture at least 19 percent more for appliances, according to a report by Trade Partnership Worldwide for the National Retail Federation. That means a typically-$200 coffee maker could have an inflated sticker price that runs you at least $38 more. The Budget Lab at Yale University estimates that the current tariff policies will cost households about $1,800 on average in 2025.

One way to manage these higher prices is to look for secondhand and refurbished items. If your local thrift store isn’t well stocked, you can still find treasure in Amazon’s Renewed selections, Wayfair’s Open Box deals outlet, eBay’s Refurbished section, Etsy’s vintage listings, and more. Not only will you do a service to your wallet, but you’ll also save some perfectly-good products from landfills. We’ve found several like-new and certified-refurbished gifts to purchase in the categories that are most targeted by tariffs on goods imported from China, Canada, and other countries.
