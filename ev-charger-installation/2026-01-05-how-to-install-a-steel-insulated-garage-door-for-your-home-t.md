Understanding Steel Insulated Garage Doors

Steel insulated garage doors are a popular choice for homeowners seeking a balance of strength and energy efficiency. These doors consist of multiple layers, typically including an outer steel shell, insulation, and an inner steel or vinyl backing. Understanding each of the components of a steel insulated garage door can help you decide if they’re the best option for your home.

On a recent This Old House project, Bob explained the construction of a premium steel insulated door: “It’s steel inside and out, filled with polyurethane, CFC-free foam to give it the R-16 value. And between each section is double weather seal to stop any air infiltration. The bottom of the door conforms to the unevenness of some floors—seals it up tight.” These details—continuous foam insulation sandwiched between steel skins, double weather seals between sections, and a conforming bottom seal—are hallmarks of a well-built insulated steel garage door.

According to This Old House Magazine, steel garage doors are the most popular option because they are strong, long lasting, and virtually maintenance-free. Look for dent-resistant 24-gauge sections, as thinner steel is more prone to damage. Foam-insulated steel doors can achieve R-values as high as 17, making them a top performer for energy efficiency among all garage door materials.

Steps for Installing a Steel Insulated Garage Door

Prep the bottom panel by attaching any necessary rollers and hinges.

Regular maintenance can extend the life of your steel insulated garage door and ensure optimal performance. A well-maintained door operates smoothly and reduces the risk of unexpected repairs. Routine Inspections

Perform these checks every few months to keep your garage door in top condition:

Examine springs, cables, rollers, and pulleys for signs of wear. Tighten any loose hardware.

Test the door’s balance to ensure it is functioning properly and safely.

Pro Tip: Bernie Udell, owner of Middlesex Overhead Doors in Burlington, Massachusetts, notes: “If a garage door is properly maintained with quality components, it’ll last virtually forever.” A couple of hours of work twice a year can ensure that jammed rollers or broken springs are rare occurrences. Cleaning and Lubrication

Clean the door panels with mild soap and water to maintain appearance and prevent rust.

Lubricate moving parts with a silicone-based lubricant annually to ensure smooth operation.

Avoid using grease, as it can attract dirt and grime, leading to potential issues. Addressing Common Issues

Even with regular maintenance, garage doors can encounter issues. Addressing them promptly keeps your door operating effectively:

If the door becomes noisy, check and lubricate the rollers and hinges.

If the door does not open or close properly, check the alignment of the sensors and tracks.
